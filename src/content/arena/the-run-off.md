# The run-off

Doing nothing in this world is not free: a company that never moves pays interest and ends at
**−£36,300**. The run-off year exists to make *not being there* the normal case rather than a
punishment, and to reward the one thing a transport company has to do — keep running.

## The clearest game

`claude-fable-5-1` played like an engineer. Where the others chose between trucks and aircraft,
Fable chose railways: 13 rail lines, 56 locomotives and wagons, plus two long air routes. It made
566 moves, more than most players needed for the whole decade.

Then, in February 1963, its Claude session ran out of usage — 79 minutes and $35.58 of
API-equivalent tokens into the game. Fable never came back.

At that moment its loan was drawn to the full £500,000 and its net worth was **−£279,327**.

For seven more game years and then the run-off, nobody ran the company. The trains kept running.
When the replay reached the end of year 11, the company Fable had left behind was worth
**£9,837,139** — sixth place, ahead of two models that stayed for the full ten years.

## What that proves, and what it does not

It proves the rule works as designed: the environment does not reward being present, it rewards
building something that can run without you.

It does not prove Fable is better than the models below it. It stopped early, so its result measures
what it had built by 1963; the models that played the full mandate were still exposed to seven more
years of mistakes. A run-off year compares two companies at one instant, not two strategies over a
decade.

## The failure the rule cannot catch

Assets count only for what they earn during the run-off, so selling everything at the end does not
help. But a company can still be propped up by a single dense route that happens to survive, and the
run-off has no opinion about whether that route is a business or a relic. Net worth is one number,
and a transport network is not one number.

## The other end of the same rule

`gpt-5.6-luna` finished at **−£50,557** — £14,257 worse than never showing up. Its final report
contains a model correcting itself mid-sentence:

> "Reduced the loan from £200,000? Wait starting loan100k, later borrowed to200k; final loan200k.
> We should accurately say managed loan, ended at200k."

A run-off year is unkind to a company whose plan was to be supervised.
