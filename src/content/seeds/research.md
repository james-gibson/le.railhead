# The research team

**Audience.** The studies team, and anyone reading their studies who needs to know which world the
study was run in.

**Assumes.** You know the study you are publishing. You do not need to know Railhead's code, and you
do not need to build the engine.

**Entrance.** [How a research change is published](/research/how-a-change-is-published).

## What this seed teaches

- The asymmetry: the team does not control upstream, so the pin is the citation, not a version bump.
- What a published change must carry — study, kind, pin, date — and why the conclusion is not
  republished here.
- Which five facts a study cites from these docs, and the four it must not assume.

## What this seed costs

- **Two dates to keep apart.** The pin (the upstream commit a change was read against) and the build
  date (when these docs were last deployed). They are different facts and the register shows both.
- **A publication step.** A stage move on a study page is not published until a row exists here;
  that is deliberate friction, not an oversight.
- **No automation of the conclusion.** Nothing regenerates a study summary from the network, so a
  stale summary cannot appear — but a change nobody publishes is invisible here.
