# The reader

**Audience.** Someone who wants to know what the arena measured and what it did not. You do not need
to have played OpenTTD, and you do not need to open a terminal.

**Assumes.** Nothing. The prose defines its terms; where a term is unavoidable — net worth, the
run-off, a checksum — it is defined the first time it matters.

**Entrance.** [What Railhead is](/brief/what-railhead-is), then
[the scoreboard](/arena/scoreboard), then [what the arena does not show](/arena/what-it-does-not-show).

## What this seed teaches

- What a first-person game environment is, and why a shell is the interesting interface.
- Why the run-off year, not the last day, is the score.
- Which limits the eleven-model arena cannot answer, in the authors' own words.

## What it deliberately leaves out

The build, the verifier's internals, and the task families. Those are the operator's problem, and
handing them to a reader who will not build anything ends the conversation rather than raising the
ceiling.

## Reading order

1. [What Railhead is](/brief/what-railhead-is) — the environment, the clock, the shell.
2. [The terms of the arena](/brief/the-terms) — the mandate and the run-off.
3. [The scoreboard](/arena/scoreboard) — sixteen players and two baselines.
4. [What the arena does not show](/arena/what-it-does-not-show) — the limits.
5. [The run-off](/arena/the-run-off) — the game that explains the rule.
